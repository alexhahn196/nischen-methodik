## 1. Narration pacing — MEASURED, HIGH confidence

From de-overlapped, timestamped caption word streams. WPM is words ÷ total video minutes, so it already includes pauses.

| Channel | WPM (mean) | WPM range | Words/video | Narration starts | Narration covers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sleep On Physics** | **143.4** | 135.9 – 158.0 | 17,623 | **0.0–0.3 s** | 99–100 % of runtime |
| **Calm Science** | **120.3** | 118.0 – 121.8 | 15,985 | 0.3–0.5 s | 100 % |
| Calm Space | 118.7 | 112.9 – 122.5 | 15,883 | 0.6 s | 98–100 % |
| Sleepy Science | 125.0 | 99.6 – 139.0 | 17,728 | 0.5–0.6 s | 75–100 % |
| Cosmo Explains | 130.1 | 129.6 – 130.6 | 16,860 | 0.1–0.2 s | 100 % |

### Four things this table proves

**1. Narration starts within one second. Every single video. No exceptions.**
Range across all 17: **0.0 s to 0.6 s**. There is no musical intro, no logo sting, no breathing room. The first word lands essentially on frame one. HIGH confidence.

**2. The two priority channels sit at opposite ends of the pacing spectrum.**
Sleep On Physics runs **143 WPM**; Calm Science runs **120 WPM**. That is a 19 % difference — clearly audible, and clearly deliberate. Both work commercially (23,762 vs 22,296 views/video). **Pace is not what separates winners from losers in this category.**

**3. Pace is astonishingly flat within a video — nobody slows down for sleep.**
Per-10-minute WPM curves (full data in `analysis_data.json`):
- `tvB659d_oik`: 139.7, 144.3, 146.1, 143.6, 143.9, 144.0, 138.8, 147.1, 144.3, 141.5, 138.2, 143.5 — **8 WPM of drift across two hours**
- `BA59TWIMNG4`: 143.1, 140.2, 135.5, 134.0, 136.3, 135.0, 133.3, 139.5, 135.9, 135.4, 141.6, 134.0, 138.8, 137.2, 134.6, 135.5 — **flat across 165 minutes**

The only channel showing a real ramp is **Calm Science**, which starts fast and settles: `-n6HdllViRU` runs 131.1, 128.0, then 115–123 for the remaining two hours — a **−10 % slowdown that completes inside the first 20 minutes** and then holds. That is a hook-then-settle pattern, not a sleep ramp.

> **This contradicts the natural assumption that sleep content progressively slows down. It does not. Confidence: HIGH.**

**4. One channel stops narrating a third of the way from the end.**
`xg8ieJQIB70` (Sleepy Science, 2.35 M views) runs 130–136 WPM for 90 minutes, then drops to **1.0, 2.4, 2.6 WPM** — the final 30 minutes are music only. Its first `[Music]` caption tag appears at **5,412 s**, exactly where narration stops. That is 25 % of the runtime produced for near-zero marginal cost.

**This is the single cheapest length-extension trick in the category, and it is present in the highest-viewed video in the entire sample.** But note: it is a July-2025 video (position 275/290 in the catalogue) and the channel's two *recent* videos both narrate wall-to-wall. Confidence that it works: **LOW** — one old video is not evidence.

## 2. Pauses — NOT MEASURED, and here is why I am not guessing

The brief asks for pause length and pauses per minute. The obvious shortcut is to measure gaps between caption cues. **That shortcut is invalid here** and I am not using it: YouTube's auto-caption cues are rolling, overlapping windows generated by an ASR system, not sentence boundaries. Consecutive cues on these videos overlap by ~50 % of their duration, so inter-cue "gaps" measure the captioner's windowing behaviour, not the narrator's breathing.

**What I can say:** at 120 WPM with a measured mean sentence length of 12.2–13.2 words (Calm Science) a sentence takes ~6.5 s to speak. At 143 WPM with 12.1–14.9-word sentences (Sleep On Physics) a sentence takes ~6.2 s. Both channels therefore deliver roughly **9–10 sentences per minute of runtime**, which leaves very little room for long pauses. Any inter-sentence pause much above ~1 s would show up as a WPM drop, and no such drop exists.

> **Inference, MEDIUM confidence:** inter-sentence pauses in this category are **short — likely well under 1 second**, and there is no evidence of the long, dramatic 2–3 s pauses associated with meditation content. I could not verify this by listening.

## 3. Music and the audio bed — MEASURED INDIRECTLY

YouTube's auto-captioner emits a `[Music]` token when it detects music that is audible and not masked by speech. Counting those tokens and the share of one-minute bins containing at least one gives a **relative** ranking of how present the music bed is. It is a proxy, not a level measurement.

| Channel | `[Music]` tags/video | Minute coverage | First tag | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sleepy Science** | 884 – 1,104 | **74.7 %** (99–100 % on 2 of 3) | 0.6 s | Music is a **foreground element**, audible almost continuously alongside narration |
| **Sleep On Physics** | 100 – 184 | **67.7 %** | 4.6 – 140 s | Music present throughout, clearly audible but subordinate |
| Calm Space | 13 – 21 | 13.3 % | 133 – 458 s | Music quiet — only surfaces in speech gaps |
| Calm Science | 0 – 26 | 12.1 % | 158 – 375 s | Music quiet; **one video (`-n6HdllViRU`) produced zero tags across 130 minutes** |
| **Cosmo Explains** | **0** | **0 %** | — | **No music detected in any of 3 videos, 388 minutes total** |

### What follows from this

**Music prominence spans the entire possible range within one profitable niche.** The 328k-sub leader runs music loud and constant. The 22.8k-sub laggard appears to run no music at all. Two channels run it barely-there.

**Direct observation, Sleep On Physics only (Gemini, 2 videos):** *"one unchanging ambient drone"*, *"zero SFX"*. On `tvB659d_oik` the first `[Music]` tag is at 2:20 — after the hook and after the CTA — consistent with **the cold open having no music bed at all**, then a drone entering and running to 2:03:36.

> **Do you need music? Evidence says: it is not required for the category, but the two most successful channels both use it.**
> - Cosmo Explains apparently ships with no music and is the weakest performer — but it is also the only channel with burned-in captions and no CTA, so music is confounded with everything else it does differently.
> - Calm Science, a strong performer, runs music so quietly it is nearly undetectable for 87 % of its runtime. **That proves you do not need prominent music.**
> Confidence that *some* bed is advisable: MEDIUM. Confidence that *loud* music is required: LOW — actively contradicted by Calm Science and Calm Space.

**Does the music change over the video?** `[Music]` tags on Sleep On Physics distribute evenly across every decile of runtime (11/10/17/12/17/17/15/14/7/18 on `tvB659d_oik`), which is consistent with **one unchanging bed** rather than an evolving score. Gemini's direct read on `_nLpB_Ao7zw` — *"one unchanging ambient drone"* — supports that. Confidence: MEDIUM.

## 4. Voice character — NOT MEASURED. What the evidence permits.

I have **no acoustic evidence about any narrator in this sample.** What follows are the only defensible statements.

### 4.1 The audience does not perceive these voices as a problem
I read the top 20 comments on `BLPBP5BaU-0` (Sleep On Physics, 129 comments) and `lqZntA10Q5g` (Calm Science, 122 comments) — 40 comments, the ones the algorithm surfaces first.

**Zero comments mention the voice at all.** Not "AI voice", not "robotic", not "great narration", not "soothing voice". Nothing.

What viewers *did* volunteer:
> *"This documentary is excellent, but it would be even stronger with animations that visually explain the concepts being discussed."*
> *"Great script. I wish you would have had a video to go along with the script."*
> *"great content but after hearing the same thing three times I couldn't take it any more"*
> *"Taught Electronics for 20 plus years, basically as a water (liquid) analogy. Guess I owe a lot of students both an apology and perhaps a refund 😉"*
> *"Fascinating. I barely slept."*

**Interpretation, MEDIUM confidence:** in a sample of 40 self-selected top comments, complaints about visuals and repetition appear and complaints about voice do not. That is weak evidence that whatever these channels use for narration clears the audience's bar. It is **not** evidence about whether the voices are human or synthetic — I genuinely do not know, for any of the five channels.

### 4.2 Script register is documentary, not bedtime — MEASURED
The scripts themselves tell you what the delivery has to support:

| | Sleep On Physics | Calm Science | Calm Space | Sleepy Science | Cosmo Explains |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Physics jargon / 1,000 words | **26.3** | **21.3** | 3.4 | 2.4 | 5.2 |
| Flesch–Kincaid grade | 9.4 | 9.1 | 9.0 | 9.2 | 9.0 |
| Mean sentence length (words) | 14.1 | 13.0 | 12.4 | 14.1 | 14.3 |
| "you/your" per 1,000 words | 8.6 | 7.6 | 6.5 | **12.6** | **12.9** |
| Explicit sleep language ("fall asleep", "drift off", "close your eyes") | **0–5 hits/video** | **2–3** | 3–6 | **2–8** | 1–4 |

**Sleep On Physics never says the word "sleep" in a 124-minute video called *Sleep On Physics*.** Its only sleep signal is the word "Tonight" in the opening sentence. Calm Science is the same. These are **documentaries that happen to be watched at night**, and the narration must read as a documentary narrator, not a sleep guide.

Sleepy Science and Cosmo Explains go the other way — explicit greetings, explicit sleep framing, twice the second-person address.

### 4.3 What I am NOT telling you
The brief asked for a recommendation like *"male voice, 45–60 apparent age, 110–125 WPM, low pitch, minimal pitch variation, 0.5–1.5 s pauses."* **I can give you the WPM from measurement. I cannot give you gender, age, pitch, pitch variation or pause length, because I never heard these videos.** Inventing those numbers would be exactly the failure mode the brief warned against.

**How to close this gap cheaply (do this before you commit to a voice):**
1. Open 3 competitor videos in a browser, listen to 60 seconds of each at 0:30, 30:00 and 90:00.
2. Note gender, apparent age, accent, and whether you hear breaths and mouth noise (human) or uniform phrasing and identical inter-sentence timing (TTS).
3. Run any 60-second clip through a loudness meter (`ffmpeg -i clip.wav -af ebur128 -f null -`) to get integrated LUFS and loudness range for voice-plus-bed.
4. That is ~20 minutes of work and it replaces every LOW-confidence row in this document with a measurement.
