## PART 1 — Which channel should we model? (Step 20)

### If we could use only ONE channel as the primary production reference

> # **CALM SCIENCE**

**Not Calm Space (16× bigger), not Sleepy Science Channel (57× bigger), not Sleep On Physics (5× the subscribers).**

**The reasoning, from measurement:**

1. **Calm Science is the experiment you are about to run, already running.** Calm Science and Calm Space are almost certainly the same operator (identical description template, identical 20 % letterbox, verbatim-identical CTA construction, identical "Tonight, we're going to…" opener across 7/7 videos, near-identical colour and pacing statistics — see `02_channel_comparison.md` §3.1). Calm Space is a proven 93,900-subscriber **space** channel. Calm Science is that operator taking the same production template into **physics**. That is precisely your plan.

2. **It has already worked, fast.** In **29 videos** Calm Science reached **22,296 views per video** — statistically level with Sleep On Physics' **23,762**, which took **123 videos**. The template transfers to physics, and it transfers quickly.

3. **Its cadence matches yours and nobody else's.** Calm Science ships **2.89 uploads/week**. Every other channel in the sample runs 5.0–6.8. Your 3/week plan is only proven viable by this one channel.

4. **It is the cheapest strong performer to reproduce.** 331 assets/120 min (the lowest in the sample), 21.8 s per asset, near-inaudible music (12.1 % `[Music]` coverage), zero text, zero diagrams, one crossfade, opens on black. Everything about it is template-shaped.

5. **It is a physics channel, so its script metrics transfer directly.** 21.3 jargon terms per 1,000 words at grade 9.1 — as opposed to Calm Space's 3.4 and Sleepy Science's 2.4, which are storytelling, not explanation.

**The one thing not to copy from it:** its 2.39:1 letterbox and its "Tonight, we're going to…" opener are its operator's fingerprints, shared with Calm Space. Using them makes you look like their third channel.

## PART 2 — Executive answers (Step 30)

### 1. Which channel should we primarily model?
**Calm Science.** Same niche, same 3/week cadence, same cold-start position, and it is the same operator's proven space template already ported into physics — 22,296 views/video in only 29 videos. **Confidence: HIGH** (measured views-per-video, cadence, and five independent same-operator fingerprints).

### 2. Which channel should influence our scripts?
**Sleep On Physics.** 26.3 jargon terms/1,000 words at Flesch–Kincaid 9.4 — 5–11× more technical than the space channels at the same reading difficulty. Take its density, questions (11.8/h), analogies (17.9/h), attention resets (every 30–45 s) and its spiral restatement structure. Take Calm Science's slower 120 wpm delivery rather than Sleep On Physics' 143. **Confidence: HIGH** (measured across 280,000 words).

### 3. Which channel should influence our visuals?
**Sleepy Science Channel** for *composition* — one dominant object, deep negative space (edge density 0.024 vs 0.070–0.107 elsewhere), near-black frames (80.8 % dark pixels), topic-matched subjects. **But not its palette** (warm amber is its fingerprint) and **not its flat cut curve**. Take the palette from Calm Science/Calm Space (blue-violet, B−R ≈ +5.6/+6.1, saturation 0.43). **Confidence: HIGH** for the measurements; **MEDIUM** that composition is what drives its lead.

### 4. Which channel should influence our audio?
**Calm Science.** Its bed is nearly inaudible (12.1 % `[Music]` caption coverage) and it performs on par with Sleep On Physics. That means the cheap, low-risk audio build is also the competitive one: one unchanging ambient drone, entering around 60–90 s, ~20 dB under narration, zero SFX. **Confidence: MEDIUM** — this rests on caption-annotation proxies and viewer comments, not on listening. ⚠️ **Verify by ear before locking your mix.**

### 5. How should our videos look?
**Near-black 16:9 full-bleed frames** (mean luminance 28–36/255, 65–75 % of pixels below 32), **one dominant object per frame with deep negative space**, **deep blue-violet base with thin cyan measurement geometry and sparing amber accents**, saturation 0.42–0.48, **zero on-screen text**, **zero diagrams**, **zero chapters**, no faces. Every image holds ~20 seconds under a **3–5 % Ken Burns move**, joined by a **1.2 s cross dissolve**, with a persistent low-opacity particle overlay and vignette. **No letterbox.** The first three minutes are hand-cut with hard cuts at ~7 s; from minute 10 onward it is one unchanged template for 110 minutes, fading to black over the final 3–5 s. **Confidence: HIGH.**

### 6. How many unique visual assets are actually necessary?
**~350 placed assets for a 120–128 minute episode — of which only ~200 need to be newly generated.**
Measured range across 17 videos: **296–499**, mean ~350, or **24.7–41.6 assets per 10 minutes**. Per channel: Calm Science 331 · Sleepy Science 350 · Calm Space 351 · Sleep On Physics 394 · Cosmo Explains 408.
The rest comes from within-episode recycling (which competitors demonstrably do) and a growing library. **Sleep On Physics cut from ~500 to ~300 assets mid-catalogue with no visible performance penalty.**
**So: not 50, not 100, not 150. Three hundred to four hundred. Confidence: HIGH** (validated detector, full-runtime coverage, three independent ground truths).

### 7. How often should the image change?
**Every 20 seconds in the body** (measured category range **17.7–21.8 s**; ~3 changes/minute), **every 12 seconds from 3:00–10:00**, and **every 7 seconds in the first 3 minutes**. **Never hold a shot longer than 45 seconds** — 0 % of measured shots on either priority channel exceed 60 s. **Confidence: HIGH.**

### 8. What voice style should we use?
**125 words per minute (band 120–132), flat across the whole video — no progressive slowdown. First word within 1 second of frame one. Narration wall-to-wall for ~100 % of the runtime. Documentary register, not bedtime: never say "sleep"; one "tonight" at most.** All measured.
**On gender, apparent age, accent, pitch and pause length I have no evidence and give no recommendation.** Media streams were auth-gated and I never heard these videos; the multimodal tool that can hear was rate-limited before the audio pass. **Spend 20 minutes listening to three competitors yourself before choosing a voice** — see `04_audio_voice_analysis.md` §4.3 for exactly what to listen for. **Confidence: HIGH on pacing, NONE on timbre.**

### 9. Do we need music?
**A bed is advisable but must be quiet — and it is not proven necessary.** Four of five channels have one; prominence spans the entire possible range (Sleepy Science 74.7 % `[Music]` coverage, Calm Science 12.1 %, Cosmo Explains 0 %) and both extremes of the *successful* channels work. Use one unchanging ambient drone, entering at ~60–90 s (leave the hook dry), ~20 dB under narration, thinning after minute 110. **No sound effects.** **Confidence: MEDIUM** — indirect evidence only.

### 10. How complex should editing be?
**Extremely simple, and concentrated almost entirely in the first three minutes.**
- **Minutes 3–128: one template, unchanged.** One transition type (1.2 s crossfade), one motion type (3–5 % Ken Burns, random direction, ~35 % pan), one overlay, one grade. Fully machine-generated.
- **Minutes 0–3: the only human edit.** ~25 shots, hard cuts, brighter, no music.
- Cut rate steps down **−37 % to −44 %** by minute 10 on all three strong still-image channels and **never re-escalates**.
- Gemini, describing a competitor unprompted: *"two productions bolted together: a bespoke hand-cut 3-minute hook … followed by ~105 minutes of a single rigid template."*
**Confidence: HIGH.**

### 11. Can this realistically be produced for under $50?
**Yes, comfortably — and your $50 ceiling is roughly 2–3× more than the format needs.**
Marginal cost lands at **~$6 lean / ~$20 balanced / ~$46 premium** before contingency; with realistic retry margins, **$17 / $34 / $46**. **Recommended: the $35 balanced build**, spending the extra dollars on research depth and fact-checking rather than on premium TTS (zero of 40 top comments across the two priority channels mention the voice at all).
At 13 episodes/month that is **~$500/month all-in**. Against a measured RPM of **$14–20** and analogue channels averaging 22,000–24,000 views/video, break-even is around **3,200 views per video**.
**The real constraint is human hours, not money. Confidence: HIGH on cost; MEDIUM on the pricing specifics, which drift.**

### 12. What should we deliberately do differently from competitors?
**One thing, and it is the highest-value finding in this analysis:**

> ## **Make the pictures show the thing being discussed.**

Four of five channels — including **both** of your priority references — run **generic nebula wallpaper that is completely decoupled from the script**. A Sleep On Physics episode about light speed shows purple nebulae for 121 of 124 minutes. A Calm Science episode called *"The True Scale of a Single Atom"* shows only galaxies. Two viewers said so unprompted in the top comments of a video that got 79,844 views in seven days:
> *"This documentary is excellent, but it would be even stronger with animations that visually explain the concepts being discussed."*
> *"Great script. I wish you would have had a video to go along with the script."*

Your **cutaway / cross-section / thin-cyan-measurement-geometry** identity delivers exactly this, is present on **none** of the five channels, and **costs the same as generating nebulae — it is a prompt-library decision, not a budget decision.**

Also deliberately differ on: **no "Tonight, we're going to…" opener** (used by 9 of 17 sampled videos) · **no letterbox** (Calm Science/Calm Space's fingerprint) · **no explicit sleep language** · **no verbal channel greeting** · **no burned-in captions** · **blue-violet not amber** (amber is Sleepy Science's).

And note where your own plan needed adjusting: **"nearly black backgrounds + one dominant object + deep negative space + warm amber" is a precise description of Sleepy Science Channel**, the 328,000-subscriber market leader. That part of your identity is not differentiation — it is the house style of the biggest competitor. The cutaways and measurement geometry are the genuinely new part. **Lead with those.** **Confidence: HIGH.**

### 13. What is the final recommended production stack?
**Research** NexLev MCP + `yt-dlp` · **Script** Claude Sonnet-class, one movement per call · **Fact check** a second model + a mandatory full human read · **Voice** ElevenLabs Turbo *or* Azure/Google Neural2 (A/B them — no evidence premium buys anything) · **Images** FLUX schnell for ~175 bulk + FLUX dev for ~25 hero, generated at 3840×2160 · **Motion & assembly** FFmpeg `zoompan` + `xfade` driven by Python · **Intro only** DaVinci Resolve free · **Audio** FFmpeg `loudnorm`/`acompressor` to −14 LUFS · **Music** Epidemic Sound or a CC0 pack · **Captions** Whisper → SRT, uploaded as a track, never burned in · **Orchestration** Python + a JSON manifest per episode. **Total software cost: $0.** Full rationale and rejected options: `11_production_stack.md`.

### 14. How many human hours per video after optimisation?
**~3.9 hours**, down from ~8.3 on the first few episodes. Machine time runs ~3.5–6.5 hours unattended and overlapping.
**About 2 of those 4 hours are irreducible:** reading all 16,000 words (~64 min minimum), scanning ~200 generated images (~20 min), and watching 8 QC timestamps (~20 min). At 3 videos/week that is **~12 hours per week.** Batching all three episodes through each stage together is what makes that number achievable. **Confidence: MEDIUM** — this is a build estimate, not a measurement of competitors.

### 15. What is the single biggest production mistake to avoid?
> ## **Shipping a physics error inside the spiral.**

The format restates its core claim **4–12 times per video** — that is measured, and it is the structural device that makes these videos work for a listener drifting in and out. It also means **one wrong mechanism gets repeated up to a dozen times in a single episode**, and the comment sections of these channels are full of working physicists, engineers and retired teachers who correct them.

The defence costs about one hour: a second-model fact-check pass over every number, name and date, then **read the whole script yourself before it goes to TTS**. It is the one hour in this entire pipeline you must never automate away.

*(Runner-up, and the one most likely to bite in practice: **repetition** — visual and verbal. A viewer of the leading physics channel wrote, in the top comments, "after hearing the same thing three times I couldn't take it any more." Guard it with the automated 8-gram and perceptual-hash checks in `13_quality_control_checklist.md`.)*

## PART 3 — Where the evidence contradicted the brief's assumptions

The brief asked to be told this plainly. Five places:

| Assumption in the brief | What the measurements show |
|---|---|
| *"whether visual intensity changes over time"* — implied gradual | It is a **cliff, not a slope**: −37 % to −44 % completed by minute 10, then dead flat for 110 minutes. Concentrate effort in the first 10 minutes, not in a gradual taper |
| *Sleep content presumably slows down as it goes* | **It does not.** WPM is flat to within 8 wpm across two hours on every channel. Only Calm Science slows, by 10 %, and it finishes doing so by minute 20 |
| *"nearly black backgrounds, one dominant object, negative space, amber highlights"* as **our** identity | This is a frame-by-frame description of **Sleepy Science Channel**, the 328k-sub leader. It is the category's house style, not differentiation. Your cutaways and measurement geometry are the new part |
| *"$50 maximum, $20–40 ideal"* | The ceiling is **2–3× higher than needed**. Marginal cost is $6–20 fully automated. The binding constraint is **human hours** |
| The brief's proposed script pattern (Question → Context → Explanation → Analogy → Implication → New question) | Close, but the measured structure is a **spiral, not a chain** — and its load-bearing move is a step the brief omits: **restating the core claim 4–12 times in new words**, so a listener drifting in and out can re-enter at any point |

## PART 4 — What I could not determine

Stated plainly, because these are real gaps in this analysis and not things to paper over:

1. **Everything about the sound of these videos.** Media streams were authentication-gated and I did not circumvent that. Voice gender, apparent age, accent, pitch, pitch variation, pause length, LUFS, RMS and dynamic range are **unmeasured**. Human-vs-TTS is **undetermined for all five channels**. ~20 minutes of your own listening closes this gap entirely.
2. **Whether the imagery is AI-generated, licensed stock, or processed astrophotography.** Gemini flagged AI generation on two Sleep On Physics videos; for the other 15 it is my visual judgement. What is certain — and what matters for cost — is that it is **2D stills with slow motion applied**.
3. **Any causal claim about performance.** With 3–4 videos per channel and 5 channels, correlations between production choices and views are **not statistically meaningful**. Where I say "the channel that does X performs worst", that is one channel differing on five axes at once.
4. **Audience retention.** No retention data exists for competitor channels. Nothing in this analysis infers it. §15's "retention proxies" are structural devices measurable in the artefacts, nothing more.
5. **The true cut rate of the first 60 seconds** on the three strongest channels. The 10-second storyboard sampling saturates there. The reported ≥6–7 changes/min is a **lower bound**; Gemini's direct shot lists on two videos suggest the real figure is ~8–9 changes/min (6.4–7.5 s per asset).
